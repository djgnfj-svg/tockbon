# Grassland, the whole loop

**Status**: `1.ready` — designed, nothing built

**The instruction that shapes this doc** (from the user, 2026-08-13): **build the biggest loop first.**
Not parts assembled upward. The last game's loop never ran end to end, and the user said in as many words
that not knowing *what it feels like* was the frustrating part. So the first build is **one stage entered
bare and left with a finished body**, thin everywhere, rather than any one system done properly.

**What it is built on**: the prototype in `src/` already runs the host, the swarm, scatter and rally,
carrying, the ecosystem and the level-up card pick — and **the user played it and confirmed the fun**
(`3.done/proto-round-trip`). This is not a rewrite. It is the round trip plus everything the stage needs to
end.

## The loop it has to close

Enter alone → grow the swarm with `F` → eat, level up, take parts → force passes the apex → hunt the apex →
`V` the whole swarm → the stage ends.

**The stage ending is the part that has never existed.** Everything before it half-exists already.

## What has to be built for it to close

| **the outline body** | the GDD's *Screen* | Drawn by code, not sprites. Line and dot, empty inside |

## What is deliberately left thin

**None of these blocks the loop, and all of them are open in the design docs**: what a species trait gives ·
each set's threshold · what fantastical beasts, chimeras and mutants pay · the field size and whether a
minimap is needed · colour · the final boss · every number past the placeholders.

⇒ **Do not stop to fill them.** `CLAUDE.md`: skeleton first, flesh later. The numbers in *First numbers*
exist so the build has something to run, and they are expected to move on the first session.

## Acceptance

**The user plays one grassland run start to finish** and says whether the shape of it works — splitting on
purpose, losing force to a clone that died out there, and the moment the elephant stops being a wall.

**Nothing here is accepted.** Every line above came out of planning conversations on 2026-08-13, and
planning principle 2 says planning cannot judge this.
