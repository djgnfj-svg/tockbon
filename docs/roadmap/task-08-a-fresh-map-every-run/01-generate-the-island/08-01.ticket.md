## Implementation plan

**The board half is built. This plan is the 3D half, and its whole trick is the ORDER: the drawn
island is rebuilt out of blocks BEFORE the generator is plugged in.** That way the look is proved
unchanged against a picture that already exists, instead of being judged on an island nobody has
seen.

### The one thing that makes this safe

⚠⚠ **Stage 1's test is: feed the DRAWN island's own board to the assembler and compare what comes
out against the baked mesh.** If they match, the look is preserved by construction and every later
stage inherits that. If they do not, the round stops there. **No generated island is looked at until
this passes.**

### Seams

**`src/sim/` for the choosing, `src/view/` for the standing** — the two the glossary already names,
and no new one. Which block stands on a 칸 is pure data (a board in, a list out), so a net drives it
with `.new()` and hundreds of seeds. Putting the block on screen is the view's, measured the way the
view is measured — pooled node state and committed surface counts.

### The kit is finished and the code never rotates

**The kit was completed in Blender first, on purpose.** 「Which block」 is decided by which sides of a
칸 are OPEN — a neighbouring 칸 that is lower, or water, or off the board — and that is 32 combinations
over two storeys. **The kit held 26.** The missing six were made by turning existing blocks in Blender
and baking the turn into the mesh, and the stair's other three climb directions the same way.

⚠⚠ **This is why: the deleted bake DID stamp rotated blocks, and the coast ring opened in six places
and the sea drew two white bands across itself.** With all 32 present the game looks a name up and
turns nothing at run time. **The risk is spent in Blender rather than carried into the frame.**
