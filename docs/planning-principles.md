# What you carry into planning

**This file survived both resets on purpose.** What is here is not a game — it is the standard by which a
direction is judged. It does not change when the direction does.

These came out of a single day, 2026-08-12, in which the direction changed five times, and **every change
was for the same reason.** One line each. Keep it one line each when adding.

---

## 1. The hand must not idle

If there is time spent watching something roll on by itself, the game is stifling.
**This one line is why a whole tower-defence direction was folded.**

⚠⚠ **The bend was cancelled on 2026-08-25 and the principle now stands unbent** — the user played Bad
North and reversed **plan the whole landing, press start, watch**: **hands move, and squads are commanded
on the board at any time.** ⇒ Nothing is being watched roll by, and **this line is no longer being paid
against.** ⚠ **If the fight ever feels like dead air, this line is still the first thing to re-read**, not
the last.

⚠ **The record of the bend, kept because a cancelled bend argues for the principle rather than against it**:
the user's own decision on 2026-08-18 was to plan everything in front of the start button and watch — see
`plan-then-watch`. The bend was paid for by moving every decision ahead of that button.

⚠⚠ **2026-08-19 — the payment was deleted and the bend got 19–29× wider.** `plan-then-watch` paid for the
bend with three candidates, and the only one that was concrete was **「a fight is short」**. The user then
set a stage at **10–15 minutes** and deleted the time limit. The measured fight was **31 s**. **Nothing is
built and nothing is accepted**; the arithmetic is in `push-inland`, and what it said about this line was:
the boat becomes a live control again, **but a node offers at most R landing decisions in total** —
**one action every 3.1 to 90 seconds depending on R.**
⚠⚠ **Both halves of that arithmetic are gone (2026-08-26)**: **the player places no boats** — the boats are
the beasts' — and **there are no nodes**, because the game is one island held against waves. **What the
paragraph still proves is the shape**: a plan-then-watch loop prices its own dead air, and this one priced
it at up to 90 seconds an action before anyone noticed.

## 2. Planning cannot decide whether something is fun

A day of changing direction five times answered nothing that one multi-day prototype closed the same day.

## 3. Never having finished one is the biggest hole right now

Every game has a stretch that looks unfun, and **only someone who has finished one knows it passes.**
An eight-month project was thrown away and the direction changed five times in a day. The next one goes to
the end even if it is not fun.

## 4. Four nouns and the game will not draw

Soldiers, buildings, turrets and magic circles all at once meant none of them was the protagonist, and
whichever was left over looked forced. **There are two systems.** One way of fighting, one way of building.

## 5. If it does not reduce to one sentence, it does not sell

If the pitch needs two sentences, it has already lost.

## 6. What sells is a single screenshot

Games do not sell for being creative — they sell because **one frame shows something insane.**
A plan whose one frame does not draw needs another look.

## 7. Pick the direction that does not get stuck on art

The direction that needs fewer assets wins. If one of them works with a single circle, that is the one.

## 8. A combination must have its result changed by order

A straight power-up is not a combination. In the old game **spread and explode were seen on screen to differ
by order alone** — this is the only surviving verification — **the user watched, on screen, order change the
kind of result** (spread ↔ explode).
⚠ Only that one observation is alive; the 565 lines around it were rules of a deleted game. **Read this one
line as the grounds, not any document.**
