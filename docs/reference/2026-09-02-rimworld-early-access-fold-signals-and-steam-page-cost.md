# What was RimWorld's Early Access build, what do indies measure before folding a game, and what does a Steam page cost?

**RimWorld entered Steam EA already 2.7 years and 120,000 paying players old; the usual fold signal
indies publish is 7,000 wishlists before launch (median first-week conversion 15-25%); a Steam page
costs $100 once per title, recouped at $1,000 of revenue.**

### How finished Tynan said it was — his own words on the store page

> "The game is already fully playable, balanced, and generally free of bugs. Over 120,000 players have
> been enjoying it since our Kickstarter in October 2013. However, we want to add more value before
> calling it done. We're doing Early Access to indicate that we still intend to add more content to the
> game."
>
> "To be clear - we might end development soon, or we might continue it for years. Please buy the game
> only if you want to play it as it exists now - not based on what it might some day become."

— RimWorld Steam store page, Early Access section, quoted [17 Jul 2016](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/366298942113585478/)

He framed Alpha 14 itself as a polish pass, not a feature push: "it is also a refinement-oriented update.
I could've packed more mega-features ... but instead chose to focus on fixing a lot of old issues."
([20 Jun 2016](https://ludeon.com/blog/2016/06/steam-release-on-july-15/))

## B. What indies measure before folding

### Cases

| Who | The threshold they publish | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Chris Zukowski** (howtomarketagame) | **7,000-10,000 wishlists minimum before launch** — the entry price for Steam's Popular Upcoming. Explicitly: "if you are sitting at 3000 wishlists despite at least 6 months of trying EVERYTHING ... it might be time to just release it ... It is better to spend money on a new project than spend money on ads for a game that just doesn't excite people." Also: "You still should try to get a minimum of 7000 wishlists before you release to Early Access. That is your launch." | [howtomarketagame, 26 Sep 2022, updated Jun 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/09/26/how-many-wishlists-should-i-have-when-i-launch-my-game/) |
| **Chris Zukowski** (the earlier signal) | **The first two weeks of a Coming Soon page.** Survey of 57 games: **median 149 wishlists, average 1,025.** By eventual gross revenue: 100 (bronze) / 500 (silver) / 1,200 (gold) / 7,000 (diamond). "Most games earn about the same amount in the first 2 weeks which is right around 150. This is the organic visibility that Steam gives you for free" | [howtomarketagame, 31 May 2021](https://howtomarketagame.com/2021/05/31/how-many-steam-wishlists-will-you-get-in-your-first-two-weeks/) |
| **Simon Carless** (GameDiscoverCo), on Zukowski's benchmark page | **First-week wishlist-to-sale conversion.** Sorted by revenue: $1,000 = 11.93%, $10,000 = 20.49%, $100,000 = 25.35%, $1M = 27.08%. Sorted by wishlist count: under 4,999 = 15%, 5,000-39,999 = 20%, 40,000-99,999 = 23%, 100,000+ = 25% (medians) | [howtomarketagame benchmarks](https://howtomarketagame.com/benchmarks/), study dated Jun 2020 |
| **GameDiscoverCo**, 2024-2025 data | Median first-week conversion for games with **over 25,000 wishlists is 0.15x** (15,000 sales per 100,000 wishlists); for games priced **above $10 it drops to 0.10x**. Underperformers had a median user score of 67% in week one vs 91% for overperformers, and averaged **411 days** with a live store page vs **214** for overperformers | [summary of the GameDiscoverCo report, 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-the-state-of-steam) |
| **Valve** (Erik Peterson, devcom 2023) | Valve's own line: "Wishlists. Do they have an impact on your visibility on Steam? No!" — except Popular Upcoming. On the numbers publishers quote: "developers ... claim that they've heard that you need ... maybe 50,000 or 100,000 wishlists. I'm here to tell you that that's just simply not the case." | quoted at length in [howtomarketagame, 29 Jan 2024](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/01/29/do-wishlists-matter-any-more/) |

### Before the build ships, or after?

**Both are practised, and they measure different things.**

- **Before** — the wishlist thresholds above. Zukowski's whole method is a pre-launch decision: get the
  Coming Soon page up early, read the first two weeks, read the run-up to 7,000.
- **After** — Valve's own hierarchy puts purchases above wishlists: "Purchase (high trust) - This is the
  gold standard ... The purchase metric is very simple: HOW MUCH MONEY did your game earn."
  ([Zukowski relaying Valve, 29 Jan 2024](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/01/29/do-wishlists-matter-any-more/))
- ⚠ Zukowski's conclusion when the pre-launch signal is bad is **not** "cancel" — it is "release it and
  put the money into the next game."

### Who did the opposite

| Who | What they did | How it turned out | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **poncle / Luca Galante — Vampire Survivors** | Coming Soon page up for only the required ~2 weeks; **about 90 wishlists at launch**, 1,000 Twitter followers, no launch-day tweet, asset-store art, under 10 reviews in the first month | Streamers found it in Jan 2022; Zukowski estimated **~$1.5M in just over one month**. Later a BAFTA winner | [howtomarketagame, 31 Jan 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/01/31/vampire-survivors-success-an-opportunity-in-the-steam-marketplace/) |
| **Eric Barone — Stardew Valley** | **Refused Early Access on purpose.** "It seemed like nearly every indie game was doing Early Access at the time. I wanted my game to stand out from the crowd, and I knew that many people were growing tired of paying for unfinished products. Furthermore, I felt that Early Access was better suited to very open-ended sandbox games, but was not ideal for a game like Stardew Valley." Four years solo, straight to 1.0 on 26 Feb 2016 | Steam top 10 within a week; **830,000 copies by early April 2016** | [MCV interview with Barone, 8 Apr 2016](https://mcvuk.com/business-news/consoles/how-stardew-valley-won-steam/) |
| **An unnamed $1M+ game in Zukowski's own survey** | Its Coming Soon page earned **60 wishlists** in the first two weeks | Still reached diamond tier, $1M+ gross | [howtomarketagame, 31 May 2021](https://howtomarketagame.com/2021/05/31/how-many-steam-wishlists-will-you-get-in-your-first-two-weeks/) |
| **Peak, Mage Arena, R.E.P.O.** | Sold **many times** their wishlist counts — 29.29x, 8.67x, 7.51x first-week conversion against a 0.15x median | Wishlist count did not predict them at all | [GameDiscoverCo summary, 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-the-state-of-steam) |

## C. What a Steam store page costs, read 2026-09-02

| Question | Answer | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Fee | **$100 USD per app**, paid once per title | [Steamworks: Steam Direct Fee](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/appfee) |
| Per-title or per-account | **Per title.** "you can now simply pay a $100 USD (or equivalent) fee for each new app". Paying attaches an **app credit** to the paying person's Steam account, activatable only by them | same |
| Refundable | **No.** "not refundable, but will be recoupable in the payment made after your product has at least $1,000.00 Adjusted Gross Revenue for Steam Store or in-app purchases" | same |
| If never released | Never reaches $1,000 AGR, so **never recouped** — the $100 is spent. Valve's docs describe no expiry or reclaim of an unactivated credit | absence noted, not stated by Valve |
| Coming Soon page free? | **Yes** — no separate charge. It collects wishlists and opens the Community Hub as soon as it is visible | [Steamworks: Coming Soon](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/coming_soon) |
| Minimum time up | **At least two weeks before releasing**, for new products | same, and [Onboarding](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding) |
| Maximum time up | None. "There doesn't appear to be a strong downside to having a store page up for a long time ahead of release, unless the game changes a lot ... Players who wishlist your game two years ahead of release can be just as likely to purchase as someone who wishlisted two weeks ahead" | [Steamworks: Coming Soon](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/coming_soon) |
| Waiting period | **30 days between paying the fee and being able to release** | [Steamworks: Onboarding](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding) |
| Review | 1-5 days for a store page or a build, done by Valve | same |

### Mandatory vs optional

**Mandatory, no way around it** ([Onboarding](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding)):

- Bank account details, in the same legal name used at onboarding. "If you don't yet have a bank account
  established for your business, you will need to open one before you can proceed."
- Tax questionnaire, which sets the withholding rate.
- Legal identification of the signing entity; NDA and Steam Distribution Agreement signed digitally.
- **Content Survey** — all three sections (general content, mature content, **generative AI content**)
  "must be completed prior to submitting the game to the Review Process"
  ([Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)).
- An age rating **for Germany**, since 15 Nov 2024 — but it is **free**: Valve issues one from the content
  survey, and a paid USK rating is only one of the two routes
  ([Age Ratings Mandatory in Germany](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey/germany)).

**Not a cash cost**: the survey generates regional board ratings from your answers. No rating-board fee is
required to have a page live.

**Tax on the fee itself**: VAT/GST may be added to the $100 depending on country, and the invoice is
issued to the individual Steam account that paid.
